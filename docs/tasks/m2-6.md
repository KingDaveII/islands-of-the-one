## Why

GAME DESIGN's "Building upgrades" (David, 2026-10-15; Valheim's workbench is the model): "A station is upgraded by building pieces around it, as physical objects in the world, not by pressing a button."
- A named plot is a 6 × 6-tile area with its station (the signpost) at the centre, and upgrade pieces stand inside that area.
- "A station's level is 1 plus the number of different pieces standing in its plot (each kind counts once)."
- Each piece is one data entry: station kind, materials, footprint, skill level needed, Writ line needed, and effect. "Until then every piece gives a placeholder +25% to its station's cycle speed (additive), so levels are felt from day one."
- Limits: "Two pieces per station per Writ line." A piece that needs a higher Writ line or skill level shows in the list with what it needs.
- "Pieces can be moved any time for free, and taken down to get every material back into the store: nothing is ever destroyed here."
- "Pieces fade with their plot and return with it."

LORE named the pieces and wrote their cards (Lore §16.8, 2026-10-18; "A card says what a piece is, never what it does: the effect line is GAME DESIGN's and is shown apart from it"). In M2 only the field exists (אדמה is the only Ground word), so only the field's pieces can be built.

Everything player-facing below is LORE's or GAME DESIGN's, word for word, except the lines marked **(proposed)**. MAIN has sent those, and the open numbers, to LORE and GAME DESIGN today. Put every line and number in data, or in one constants module the tests read, so their answers are an edit.

## Decisions (MAIN)

### 1. Data

A new `packages/shared/data/pieces.json`, validated on load (`validatePiecesData`). Each piece has:
- `id`: kebab-case and permanent (stored in the DB);
- `stationKind`: a plot kind of stations.json;
- `name` and `card`: LORE's, word for word;
- `cost`: items and counts, taken from the store;
- `footprint`: `{ cols, rows }` in tiles, before rotation;
- `skill`: `{ skill, level }`, or null;
- `writLine`: the Writ line needed, 1 or more;
- `effect`: `{ kind: "cycle-speed", permille: 250 }` (GAME DESIGN's placeholder).

The field's three (Lore §16.8; costs from GAME DESIGN's "Starting pieces"):

| id | name | card (LORE) | cost | footprint | skill | Writ line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `clay-channel` | Clay channel | Fired clay troughs laid between the furrows, the way the Kingdom waters its fields. Dew gathers in them overnight, from nowhere you can find. | 300 `twisted-log`, 150 `black-stone` | 1 × 4 **(proposed)** | none **(proposed)** | 1 |
| `seed-jars` | Seed jars | Squat grey jars, waxed shut and pressed with your thumb. Seed kept dry keeps its name. | 200 `twisted-log`, 40 `pale-grain` | 2 × 1 **(proposed)** | none **(proposed)** | 1 |
| `threshing-floor` | Threshing floor | A ring of flat stones beaten smooth, where grain is trodden out of the straw. The Scattered say the oldest temples stand on threshing floors, because that is where the wind came down to choose. | GAME DESIGN gave none: 400 `black-stone`, 100 `pale-grain` **(proposed)** | 3 × 3 **(proposed)** | none **(proposed)** | 2 |

The threshing floor needs Writ line 2, which nobody has in M2, so it always shows locked with what it needs. That is GAME DESIGN's "shows in the list with what it needs".

The other stations' pieces (lumber camp, quarry, forge; Lore §16.8) wait for their Ground words. Put LORE's names and cards in `pieces.json` anyway, with `"available": false` and no cost, so the data is in one place. The validator allows a missing cost only when `available` is false, and the build route refuses such pieces as `unknown-piece`.

Every name and card passes `DRAWABLE_CHARS` and the banned-names test.

### 2. Rules (shared, pure)

Put them in `packages/shared/src/pieces.ts`, so the server and the ghost use the same function:
- `plotLevel(pieces)`: 1 + the distinct piece kinds standing.
- `cycleSpeedPermille(pieces)`: 1000 + the sum of the standing pieces' effects (each kind counts once; +250 each today).
- `cycleLengthMs(cycle, pieces)`: `round(cycleMs × 1000 / speed)`. Wire these to the plot's real pieces, replacing the zero-arguments in Task 5's seams.
- `pieceLimit(writLines)`: 2 × the Kindled's Writ lines (2 in M2).
- **`placementProblems(layout, plot, pieces, candidate)`**: the reasons a footprint (col, row, rotation 0 | 90 | 180 | 270, where 90 and 270 swap cols and rows) can't stand there. The reasons:
  - **outside:** not wholly inside the plot's 6 × 6;
  - **on-signpost:** over the signpost's tile or its interact tile;
  - **overlaps:** over another piece of that plot (a piece being moved doesn't block itself);
  - **blocks-signpost:** it would leave the signpost's interact tile unreachable. Check with a flood fill over the layout's walkable tiles, with every piece and named-plot signpost solid, from the layout's start.

  An empty list means it fits. The ghost is green exactly when the list is empty.
- Each kind may stand **once** per plot **(proposed)**: "each kind counts once" in the level suggests duplicates add nothing, so M2 doesn't allow them. GAME DESIGN has been asked.

### 3. Server

- **Storage:** a new table, `plot_pieces` (the Kindled, `plot_id`, `piece_id`, `col`, `row`, `rotation`, `built_at`; primary key on the Kindled, the plot and the piece; cascade). Expand-only, and only the holding module touches it.
- **Three routes,** each a holding request (the Kindled's lock, the Writ's, the plots' rows, the settle, then the act), each answering the holding:
  - `POST /api/plots/build`, `{ plotId, pieceId, col, row, rotation }`. It refuses, in order:
    - `unknown-plot`, `unnamed-plot`, `faded`;
    - `unknown-piece` (400: not in pieces.json, not available, or not this plot's kind);
    - `already-built` (409);
    - `needs-line` (409);
    - `needs-skill` (409);
    - `limit` (409: the plot has `pieceLimit` pieces);
    - `blocked` (400: `placementProblems` not empty; the message names the first reason);
    - `insufficient` (409: the store lacks the cost; the pack doesn't count, GAME DESIGN: "the materials leave the store").

    The cost leaves the store through `inventory.apply`, with a new reason `build`.
  - `POST /api/plots/move`, `{ plotId, pieceId, col, row, rotation }`: free. It refuses `unknown-plot`, `unnamed-plot`, `faded`, `not-built` (409) and `blocked`.
  - `POST /api/plots/take-down`, `{ plotId, pieceId }`: every material back into the store, through `inventory.apply` with reason `build` and positive deltas. It refuses `unknown-plot`, `unnamed-plot`, `faded`, `not-built` and `store-full` (409): a refund that doesn't fit the store's stack limit is refused whole, with the take-all's line, "Store: twisted logs full.", so nothing is ever destroyed.
- **The trickle:** a change to a plot's pieces is an act after the settle. The settle credits the cycles up to now at the old speed, and `cycled_until` stays where it is: the partial cycle carries on at the new speed. Test that a stretch either side of a build pays at its own speed.
- **The holding answer:** `plots[]` gains `pieces` (`[{ pieceId, col, row, rotation }]`) and the real `level`; `cycles[...]`'s `cycleMs` is the plot's real length. Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` (6).
- **Faded plots** keep their pieces (frozen with the plot) and refuse every build route as `faded`. A rename that changes the Ground (only the rule in M2) takes every piece down with a refund, in the rename's transaction; that refund may overfill the store.
- **A playtest CLI:** `plots:pieces <user> <plotId>` lists a plot's pieces. With `--clear`, it takes them all down with refunds.

### 4. Client

- **The way in:** the field's station panel gains **BUILD HERE** (GAME DESIGN: "Use a station → Build here opens build mode"). It isn't shown while faded or not sown.
- **Build mode** (`ui/buildMode.ts`, its logic in `game/buildView.ts`, pure and tested).
  - **The world:** the plot's ring is drawn on the ground; the camera stays on the plot.
  - **The side list, on the right:**
    - the pieces this station can take, each with a placeholder picture (its footprint drawn as tiles in a palette token with its initials);
    - its name;
    - its materials with what the store holds ("Twisted logs 340/300" **(proposed)**, short items in the warning token);
    - what it needs, if anything ("Needs Writ line 2" **(proposed)**, "Needs Farming 5" **(proposed)**);
    - its effect line, apart from the card: "+25% speed" **(proposed)**;
    - LORE's card in its tooltip.
  - **The header:** "Field · level 2" **(proposed)** and the pieces against the limit, "Pieces 1/2" **(proposed)**.
- **Placing.**
  - Picking a piece makes a **ghost** of its footprint follow the pointer (or, on a pad, a tile cursor moved by the left stick or the D-pad), snapped to the tile grid.
  - The ghost is drawn green where `placementProblems` is empty and red where it isn't. Use placeholder tokens, never the reserved signal colours (readability.md), and list both under "Questions for ART".
  - The client also reds a footprint over the Kindled's own tile. Positions are the client's, so the server can't check that one.
  - **Rotate:** a new `rotate` action in `controls.json`: R on the keyboard; on the pad, Triangle in the Calm context **(proposed)**. Check it against the uniqueness test and pick another free button if Triangle is taken.
  - **Confirm** (LMB, Enter, Cross) sends the route.
  - **Cancel** (RMB, Esc, Circle) drops the ghost; a second cancel leaves build mode.
- **The building moment:** when the server's answer lands, a short whole-frame scene plays on the new piece: a few dust pixels, then the piece rising from its base row by row. It runs about 400 ms, timed in `tuning.json` under `plots.buildMs`. Nothing draws before the answer.
- **Standing pieces** draw as placeholder props (a footprint-sized box in a palette token with the piece's initials, outlined), y-sorted with the bodies. Their tiles are pillars in the roam: extend `setSolidPlots` to take the pieces' tiles too.
  - A faded plot's pieces draw dim, like its ground.
  - In build mode, selecting a standing piece (click it, or the cursor on it and Cross) offers **MOVE** (it becomes the ghost; confirming sends `move`) and **TAKE DOWN** (sends `take-down`; the refund floats "+300" over the signpost, as a take-all does).
- **The panel and the level.** The field's station panel shows the real level ("Level 2"), and the plot sentence panel's "Pieces: none" becomes the pieces' names, comma-separated **(proposed)**. "By itself: …" shows the real cycle, so with one piece the field reads "1 pale grain every 12 min".
- **Refusals,** one line in the side list's footer **(proposed)**:
  - "Not enough in the store.";
  - "That doesn't fit there.";
  - "This plot can take no more pieces.";
  - "Needs Writ line 2." / "Needs Farming 5.";
  - and the take-all's "Store: twisted logs full." for `store-full`.
