## Why

David wants to playtest on his phone. Today a phone can log in, create a Kindled and tap menus, panels and build mode, but it can't walk on the holding or fight in a run: there is no stick and no attack button. This task adds touch as a third input device, next to keyboard+mouse and the gamepad, and makes every M2 screen playable with fingers alone, in landscape.

GAME DESIGN's touch column (Game design → Controls, decided with M1's control scheme) is the model:

| Action | Touch |
| --- | --- |
| Move, dodge direction | Floating left stick, anchored where the thumb lands |
| Aim | Floating right stick; same soft lock when near center |
| Light attack | Big button under the right thumb |
| Heavy attack (hold to charge) | Second big button, left of light |
| Dodge | Button above light |
| Ability (M1: one slot) | Arc of up to three small buttons above the right stick |
| Consumable 1 / 2 | Two slots, top right |
| Interact | Tap the thing, or the prompt |
| Inventory | Pack button, bottom right |
| Menu | Gear, top right |

GAME DESIGN's rules: "No action needs more than one input … Dodge direction is always the move input, never the aim … Touch uses the same model: the right stick aims, two big buttons under the right thumb are light and heavy, dodge and the ability arc sit above them, buttons at least 56 px at 1×, and the HUD leaves the top center clear for the room."

## Decisions (MAIN)

### 1. Touch is a device, and the sim never knows

- `activeDevice.ts`: `Device` gains `'touch'`. A pointer down with `pointerType === 'touch'` switches to it; a key, a mouse button or mouse motion (as today) switches to keyboard; a pad switches to gamepad. Pure and tested, like the rest of the tracker.
- Touch feeds the same `Controls.sample` as the pad: the left stick gives the move vector (through `quantizeMove` with the stick deadzone), the right stick the aim (the pad's rule: deflected aims directly, centered keeps the soft lock), and the buttons are presses and holds exactly like pad buttons, latched between ticks (§4 of controls.md). The roam's walk/run rule for a stick (`roam.walkStickMax`) applies to the touch stick unchanged.
- Nothing under `packages/sim` changes, the replay goldens don't move, and the input log records the same resolved actions.

### 2. The page on a phone

- Multi-touch: enough active pointers for two thumbs plus one (Phaser's `input.activePointers`, or `addPointer`).
- No browser gestures over the game: `touch-action: none` on the canvas and the page, `overscroll-behavior: none`, no long-press callout or text selection (`-webkit-touch-callout`, `user-select`), no double-tap zoom. The DOM form inputs (`formFields.ts`) must keep working: test the login and the island's Name again on the emulated phone.
- **Landscape.** On a touch device in portrait, show a full-screen line instead of the game, "Turn your phone sideways." (GAME DESIGN may reword it), in BitmapText on the Hollow, and keep the scene paused under it. Rotating back resumes where it was.
- **Full screen:** on the first touch in the game, ask for full screen where the browser allows it (`requestFullscreen` on Android; ignore a refusal), and lock to landscape when full screen allows it. iOS Safari has no full-screen API for pages: add a web app manifest (`display: "fullscreen"`, `orientation: "landscape"`, the name, `background_color` the Hollow) and the `apple-mobile-web-app-capable` and status-bar meta tags, so "Add to Home Screen" opens the game full screen. No icons yet (ART's question 4).
- Notches: keep the default viewport (no `viewport-fit=cover`), so the browser keeps the game inside the safe area.

### 3. The touch layer

One module, `apps/client/src/ui/touchControls.ts`, drawn on the UI camera, its logic pure in `game/touchLayout.ts` (layout, zones, stick math) and tested in node.

- **Sizes are in CSS px** (the thumb's size doesn't change with the screen), converted to UI px at layout and rounded to whole UI px: every button at least `touch.buttonMinCssPx` (56) across, the stick's radius `touch.stickRadiusCssPx` (48). All numbers live in a new `touch` block in `controls.json` (positions as offsets from the screen's corners in CSS px), never in code.
- **Zones:** a touch that starts on a button is that button's; on a HUD zone (pack, chip, Writ, panels) it is theirs, as today; on the left half of the screen anywhere else it anchors the **left stick** where it lands (the base stays there until release; dragging past the radius clamps the knob, it doesn't drag the base); on the right half anywhere else it anchors the **right stick** (runs) or is a **tap** (the holding, below). One finger owns one control until it lifts.
- **Drawing:** placeholders in palette tokens until ART's look (question 1): the stick a 1 px ring with a dithered fill and a knob, shown only while held; the buttons outlined discs with a short BitmapText label (LIGHT, HEAVY, DODGE, HAUL, the item counts), a pressed state and the Haul's cooldown as the pad hint shows it today. Never an alpha fade (CLAUDE.md). The whole layer shows only while the active device is touch.

### 4. The holding (Calm)

- The left stick roams (walk, then run, as the pad).
- **Interact:** a tap (down and up within `touch.tapMaxMs` and `touch.tapMaxCssPx`) on the right half, on the thing, or on the USE prompt, is Use, as a tap is today. The prompt becomes a tappable button on touch.
- **Menu:** the gear, top right, is the `menu` binding (Esc): it closes the open panel, else leaves for the start screen, as `back()` does.
- **Panels:** every panel (station, plot, Writ, rack, talk box, build mode, the away card) must be usable and closable by touch. A tap outside an open panel closes it (Circle's meaning); the talk box keeps advancing on a tap. Build mode keeps §12's touch rules (tap to place, "Tap again to build", the TURN button).
- The pack button and the chip are already tappable: check them.

### 5. Runs (Combat and Calm)

- Left stick moves; right stick aims; LIGHT, HEAVY (held: charge), DODGE and the HAUL arc as in the table; the two consumable slots top right (Q and F's actions; they are the HUD's item slots made tappable if they already draw there).
- In Calm (a cleared room), Interact is a tap on the thing or the prompt, as on the holding; in Combat a right-half touch is always the right stick.
- The reticle: in touch mode it shows the pad's reticle along the aim (controls.md §3), and the OS cursor stays hidden.

### 6. Menus and hints

- Every menu screen (account, creation, start, controls, inventory, results, sleep) works by tap alone. Audit each and fix gaps; list them in the PR.
- In touch mode the key and pad hint lines hide; where a hint is the only way to learn an action, show a short touch line instead ("Tap to continue" on the results screen; GAME DESIGN may reword). The controls screen adds a third column, Touch, from GAME DESIGN's table above.
