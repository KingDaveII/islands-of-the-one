## Why

David wants to playtest on his phone. Today a phone can log in, create a Kindled and tap menus, panels and build mode, but it can't walk on the holding or fight in a run: there is no stick and no attack button. This task adds touch as a third input device, next to keyboard+mouse and the gamepad, and makes every M2 screen playable with fingers alone, in landscape.

GAME DESIGN's touch column (Game design → Controls, updated on 2026-10-20 for this task) is the model:

| Action | Touch |
| --- | --- |
| Move, dodge direction | Floating left stick, anchored where the thumb lands |
| Aim | The attack and ability buttons aim: a tap fires at the move direction with a 20° soft lock; press, drag off the button and release fires in the drag direction |
| Light attack | Big button under the right thumb |
| Heavy attack (hold to charge) | Second big button, left of light: press starts the charge, drag aims, release fires |
| Dodge | Button above light; a tap, its direction from the left stick |
| Ability (M1: one slot) | Arc of up to three small buttons round light, aimed like light |
| Consumable 1 / 2 | Two slots, top right |
| Interact | Tap the thing, or the prompt |
| Inventory | Pack button, bottom right |
| Menu | Gear, top right |

GAME DESIGN's rules: "No action needs more than one input … Dodge direction is always the move input, never the aim … buttons at least 56 px at 1×, and the HUD leaves the top center clear for the room." On touch there is **no right stick** (GAME DESIGN, 2026-10-20): "On a phone the right thumb would have to choose between the stick and the buttons, the same conflict the gamepad scheme removed. The buttons are the aim." A short aim line shows from the Kindled while a button is dragged; there is no cancel gesture in M2.

GAME DESIGN and ART have open questions on this (listed at the end). Build with the defaults below; their answers come as an update to this file.

## Decisions (MAIN)

### 1. Touch is a device, and the sim never knows

- `activeDevice.ts`: `Device` gains `'touch'`. A pointer down with `pointerType === 'touch'` switches to it; a key, a mouse button or mouse motion (as today) switches to keyboard; a pad switches to gamepad. Pure and tested, like the rest of the tracker.
- Touch feeds the same `Controls.sample` as the pad: the left stick gives the move vector (through `quantizeMove` with the stick deadzone), and the buttons are presses and holds like pad buttons, latched between ticks (§4 of controls.md). The roam's walk/run rule for a stick (`roam.walkStickMax`) applies to the touch stick unchanged.
- **Aim by button** (GAME DESIGN): with no button dragged, the aim is the pad's centered-stick rule, the move direction snapped onto an enemy within `softlock.leftStickAngleDeg` (20°, the Left-stick preset's value), the previous aim kept when standing still. While a LIGHT, HEAVY or ability touch is dragged off its button (past `touch.aimDragCssPx`), the aim is the drag direction (from the button's centre to the finger), quantized like the pad's right stick.
- **When each fires:** LIGHT and an ability fire on **release** (a press at the tick the finger lifts, aimed as above; a tap that never left the button fires at the soft-locked aim). HEAVY is held from the touch down to the release, so the charge starts on press, the drag aims it and the release fires it, exactly as a held R2. DODGE fires on press. The aim line: while a button is dragged, a short dotted line from the Kindled along the aim, `touch.aimLinePx` world px long, in the Haul guide's style (controls.md §3), on the world camera.
- Nothing under `packages/sim` changes, the replay goldens don't move, and the input log records the same resolved actions.

### 2. The page on a phone

- Multi-touch: enough active pointers for two thumbs plus one (Phaser's `input.activePointers`, or `addPointer`).
- No browser gestures over the game: `touch-action: none` on the canvas and the page, `overscroll-behavior: none`, no long-press callout or text selection (`-webkit-touch-callout`, `user-select`), no double-tap zoom. The DOM form inputs (`formFields.ts`) must keep working: test the login and the island's Name again on the emulated phone.
- **Landscape.** On a touch device in portrait, show a full-screen line instead of the game, "Turn your phone sideways." (GAME DESIGN's, final), in BitmapText on the Hollow, and keep the scene paused under it. Rotating back resumes where it was.
- **Full screen:** on the first touch in the game, ask for full screen where the browser allows it (`requestFullscreen` on Android; ignore a refusal), and lock to landscape when full screen allows it. iOS Safari has no full-screen API for pages: add a web app manifest (`display: "fullscreen"`, `orientation: "landscape"`, the name, `background_color` the Hollow) and the `apple-mobile-web-app-capable` and status-bar meta tags, so "Add to Home Screen" opens the game full screen. No icons yet (ART's question 4).
- Notches: keep the default viewport (no `viewport-fit=cover`), so the browser keeps the game inside the safe area.

### 3. The touch layer

One module, `apps/client/src/ui/touchControls.ts`, drawn on the UI camera, its logic pure in `game/touchLayout.ts` (layout, zones, stick math) and tested in node.

- **Sizes are in CSS px** (the thumb's size doesn't change with the screen), converted to UI px at layout and rounded to whole UI px: every button at least `touch.buttonMinCssPx` (56) across, the stick's radius `touch.stickRadiusCssPx` (48). All numbers live in a new `touch` block in `controls.json` (positions as offsets from the screen's corners in CSS px), never in code.
- **Zones:** a touch that starts on a button is that button's; on a HUD zone (pack, chip, Writ, panels) it is theirs, as today; on the left half of the screen anywhere else it anchors the **left stick** where it lands (the base stays there until release; dragging past the radius clamps the knob, it doesn't drag the base); on the right half anywhere else it is a **tap** (below) or nothing. A touch that starts on an attack or ability button stays that button's while it is dragged off, for its aim. One finger owns one control until it lifts.
- **Drawing:** ART's look, below ("ART's answers"). The whole layer shows only while the active device is touch.

### 4. The holding (Calm)

- The left stick roams (walk, then run, as the pad).
- **Tap to walk and use** (GAME DESIGN, 2026-10-20: "On a phone, tapping a station from across the island is most of what idle play is"). A tap (down and up within `touch.tapMaxMs` and `touch.tapMaxCssPx`) anywhere on the world that isn't a control:
  - **on a thing** (a station's part, a plot, the store, the rack, the post, Kesh's rock for Talk): if the Kindled is in reach, Use it now; if not, walk to its interact point and Use it on arrival;
  - **on plain ground:** walk there (the nearest walkable tile's centre when the tile is solid or void);
  - any left-stick touch, a new tap or opening a panel stops the walk.
- **The walk** is a client autopilot, like the screenshot scripts' `walkTo` but along a path: a pure `findPath` (in `game/`, tested in node) over the roam's own room (`holdingRoomTiles`, as `RoamSession.setSolidPlots` rebuilds it with the named plots' signposts and the pieces), 8 directions with no corner cutting, and an input source that steers the roam toward each waypoint at full deflection (so it walks, then runs, as the stick does). The server is never asked where the Kindled is (`inRange` stays as it is). No path: no walk, and a thing in reach is still used.
- **The USE button** (ART's answer 3): whenever a prompt is up, a disc in the action cluster's top slot carries its verb (USE, TALK, TAKE…). On the holding the cluster holds that button alone, placed above the pack button, clear of the chip.
- **Menu:** the gear, top right, is the `menu` binding (Esc): it closes the open panel, else leaves for the start screen, as `back()` does.
- **Panels:** every panel (station, plot, Writ, rack, talk box, build mode, the away card) must be usable and closable by touch. A tap outside an open panel closes it (Circle's meaning); the talk box keeps advancing on a tap. Build mode keeps §12's touch rules (tap to place, "Tap again to build", the TURN button).
- The pack button and the chip are already tappable: check them.

### 5. Runs (Combat and Calm)

- Left stick moves; LIGHT, HEAVY, DODGE and the HAUL arc aim and fire as in §1; the two consumable slots top right (Q and F's actions; they are the HUD's item slots made tappable if they already draw there).
- In Calm (a cleared room), Interact is a tap on the thing, or the USE button. Tap-to-walk is for Calm **outside encounters** (GAME DESIGN), so in M2 the holding only: in a run's rooms a tap on the ground does nothing.
- The reticle: in touch mode the pad's reticle shows along the aim (controls.md §3), with the aim line while a button is dragged, and the OS cursor stays hidden.

### 6. Menus and hints

- Every menu screen (account, creation, start, controls, inventory, results, sleep) works by tap alone. Audit each and fix gaps; list them in the PR.
- In touch mode the key and pad hint lines hide; where a hint is the only way to learn an action, show a short touch line instead ("Tap to continue" on the results screen; GAME DESIGN's, final). The controls screen adds a third column, Touch, from GAME DESIGN's table above.

## ART's answers (2026-10-20), to build as written

**Buttons** are the plain HUD panel (no amber rim): hollow-deep face, a 1 px hollow-below bevel top and left, a 1 px dream-night outline and a hard 1 px drop shadow.
- LIGHT, HEAVY, DODGE, the Haul and USE are discs, LIGHT the largest. The Haul arc sits round LIGHT, and USE takes the cluster's top slot (above DODGE when both show).
- The two item slots and the gear are chamfered squares like the HUD's slots. An item slot draws the item's own 16 px icon from `item-icons` with its count, as the HUD does.
- **Icons, not words.** ART's touch-glyph sheet is up (#133; merge `main` into this branch once it's merged): `packages/shared/assets/touch-glyphs-16px.png` + `touch-glyphs.json`, engine sheet v2, 16 px cells, untinted, `light`, `heavy`, `dodge`, `haul`, `gear` and `use`, each with its own dream-night outline for the dithered faces. Register it in `sheets.ts` and `sheets.test.ts` and the client loader like the toast glyphs (a required sheet: the cells as listed, each 16 × 16, untinted, pixelScale 1), and draw each glyph centred on its button at 1 UI px per px. The USE button shows the hand; its verb stays on the world prompt. No word fallback is needed.
- **LIGHT's size:** ART offers a 24 px `light` cell if LIGHT ends up much bigger than the rest. Report LIGHT's diameter in UI px on the four test screens in the PR, with the others'.
- **Pressed:** the button sinks into the clay. The drop shadow goes, the content moves 1 UI px down and right, and the bevel moves to the bottom and right. No colour flash: hit white and the perfect-dodge glow stay reserved.
- **Cooldown:** as the run HUD's ability slots. A dream-night sweep darkens the time left, clockwise from 12 o'clock, with whole seconds on top, and the frame lights briefly when it's back. A locked button shows the hollow-dusk lock, as a locked slot does.

**The stick** (floating, only while held):
- the base is a 2 px ring, dream-night outside and hollow-below inside, its radius the stick's travel, with the HUD's 50 % checker dither of ui-panel inside;
- the knob is a solid panel disc with the bevel;
- both appear where the thumb lands and leave on release in one frame, with no fade.

**Coverage:** every control's fill is the 50 % checker dither of ui-panel, so every other pixel shows the world. Outlines, icons, counts and the cooldown sweep stay solid, with no alpha anywhere. The controls don't widen the camera's safe margins (on a phone that would shrink the view too much). They stay in the bottom corners, clear of the centre, as the HUD rule says; only the item slots and the gear sit top right.

**USE on touch:** the button above. The world prompt stays where it is to mark what you'd use, but on touch it shows only the verb, with no key glyph.

**Home-screen icon:** later, from ART (32 px art at 192 and 512 px, maskable, plus a 180 px apple-touch-icon). Wire the manifest now without icons; they join it when ART delivers.

**For ART, in the PR description:** the button sizes in UI px that the layout gives on the four test screens (they vary with the device, since sizes are set in CSS px), so ART draws the icons to fit.
