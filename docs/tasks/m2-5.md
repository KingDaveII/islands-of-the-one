## Why

The holding's four plots have been data since Task 3. This task makes them land you can name. A plot is named with a **plot sentence**: two word tiles, right to left. The **Ground** says what the plot is for and builds its station; the **Virtue** asks something extra of it. M2 knows one Ground word, אדמה (*adamah*, "tilled ground"), which builds a **field**. Kesh tosses you the tile after you name the island. A field makes pale grain on its own slow **basket cycle** while the Writ has charge, and faster when you farm it as your active action.

Sources: GAME DESIGN's "Holding & …" ("Harvest and the basket", "Plots with one tile", "Building upgrades"), "The Writ and erosion" ("Plot sentences"), "M2 numbers" and "M2 screens" (Plot sentence, Station basket); LORE §4.5 (the lexicon, plot seals, sigils) and §14.3 (the first plot sentence, Kesh's tile and seeds).

Everything player-facing below is LORE's or GAME DESIGN's, word for word, except the lines marked **(proposed)**. Those are MAIN's placeholders, sent to LORE today. Put every player-facing line in data (or one constants module the tests read), so LORE's answer is a text edit.

## Decisions (MAIN)

### 1. Data

- **`holding.json`, the plots.** Each plot gets:
  - an `id`: `plot-1` … `plot-4`, in today's order. Ids are permanent, because the basket is `basket:<id>` in the ledger.
  - a `station`: its `rect` (1 × 1) and its `interact` point. Put the station at the plot's centre, `rect` at (col + 2, row + 2) and `interact` at (col + 2, row + 3), so the station is the plot's signpost.

  Extend `holdingLayoutProblems`: ids unique and kebab-case; the station rect and its interact point inside the plot; the interact point ground and reachable from the start. The layout's plots in order belong to Writ line 1, `linePlots` (4) per line.
- **A new `packages/shared/data/words.json`**, validated on load (`validateWordsData`). It is the lexicon of word tiles, M2's two words only; the rest of LORE's 30 come with the tasks that give them effects. Each word has:
  - `id`, the transliteration in kebab-case (`adamah`, `yashan`; permanent, stored in the DB);
  - `letters`, base Old Letters as the Writ stores them (`shapeLetters` adds the final forms);
  - `say` ("adamah"), `meaning` ("tilled ground", "old"), `slot` (`ground` | `virtue`), `element` (`earth` …);
  - `card`, LORE's card text: אדמה: `"Tilled ground." Ground that's ready to be worked. Earth.`; ישן: `"Old." A Virtue. Things that keep their name a long time grow richer. Earth.`;

  - for a Virtue, `effect`: ישן is `{ "kind": "yield-per-day", "permillePerDay": 10, "maxPermille": 200 }` (GAME DESIGN: +1% yield for every day the plot has kept its name, up to +20%).

### 2. Known words (server)

- **Word tiles are knowledge, not items:** no slot, no ledger. Add a new table, `known_words` (the Kindled, `word_id`, `learned_at`; primary key on both; cascade). The migration is expand-only, and only the holding module writes it.
- **`POST /api/kesh/word`, `{}`.** It gives `adamah` once the island has a Name and the Kindled doesn't know it yet; otherwise it answers `not-due`. Record it in `kesh_gifts.word_given_at` (a new nullable column).
- **`POST /api/kesh/seeds`, `{}`.** It gives the seeds into the **pack** (reason `gift`, as the sliver) once a plot has been named and they haven't been given yet; otherwise `not-due`. A full pack answers `pack-full` and they stay due. Record it in `kesh_gifts.seeds_given_at`.
- **`kesh` in the holding answer** gains `wordDue` and `seedsDue` (booleans), beside the sliver's `due`. Talk plays a due gift first, as it does a due sliver: the tile line, then the seeds flow, then the sliver.

  This also covers Kindled who named their island before this task: they get the tile on their next Talk.
- **The playtest CLIs.**
  - `kesh:reset` also clears both new columns.
  - Add `words:grant <user> <wordId>` and `words:forget <user> <wordId>`, so a playtest can learn ישן, which nothing gives in M2. Tabita trades it in M4.

### 3. Plots and plot sentences (server)

- **Storage:** a new table, `plots` (the Kindled, `plot_id`, `ground`, `virtue` (null), `named_at`, `virtue_set_at` (null); primary key on the Kindled and the plot; cascade). An unnamed plot has no row. Expand-only.
- **`POST /api/plots/name`, `{ plotId, ground, virtue }`** (`virtue` may be null). It runs as a holding request: the Kindled's lock, then the settle, then the act. In order, it refuses:
  1. `unknown-plot` (400);
  2. `unnamed` (409): the island has no Name, and plots appear only once it does (LORE §14.3);
  3. `faded` (409): the plot's line has faded;
  4. `unknown-word` (400): a word not in words.json, or one the Kindled doesn't know;
  5. `wrong-slot` (400);
  6. `no-ground` (400): a sentence needs a Ground ("A Ground tile decides the building; without a Ground the plot cannot be built on");
  7. `unchanged` (409);
  8. `sentence-refused` (400): see the filter below;
  9. `insufficient` (409): a rename without the gin.

  The first naming is free. Any later change (another Virtue, filling an empty Virtue, clearing it) costs 1 `sealed-gin` from the purse through `inventory.apply`, with a new reason `name-plot`. The answer is the holding.
- **The filter.** Every plot sentence (Ground then Virtue, as the letters are written, with a space between them) goes through `isSafe(…, { strict: true })` before it is stored.

  Strict is the conservative reading of CLAUDE.md, and it has a consequence. אדמה then ישן puts he against yod across the space, so **Field (אדמה · ישן) is refused under strict**. LORE has been asked whether plot sentences take the strict rule or only the non-strict one. Keep the mode one named constant with a comment, and write the test so it enumerates every Ground × Virtue pair in words.json and pins which are refused. LORE's answer is then a one-line change and a test update. Say in the PR which pairs strict refuses today.
- **The plot's station:** a plot with a Ground is a station of its kind (the field) with id = the plot's id. `POST /api/holding/work` and `/take` accept plot ids:
  - an unnamed plot is `unnamed-plot` (409);
  - a faded plot is `faded` (409): frozen with its basket and contents (GAME DESIGN);
  - in the grace, dark but not faded, take and work are allowed: "Dark means idling stops, not that the player is locked out".

  The active action at a field settles exactly as the trees do, into `basket:plot-n`, with Farming XP. A plot's basket belongs to the plot, not its kind, so a rename never loses what it holds.

### 4. Basket cycles (server)

The passive trickle: GAME DESIGN's "one mechanism", "while the Writ is lit", "a basket holds what accumulates within the 48-hour offline cap and then waits", "basket cycles earn no XP".

- **State:** a new table, `basket_cycles` (the Kindled, `station_id`, `cycled_until`, `carry_permille`; primary key on both; cascade). A row is created when a station with a `cycle` comes into being (a plot's first naming), with `cycled_until` = now. Expand-only.
- **A holding-wide last seen.** Today `last_seen_at` lives on the activity row, which a stop deletes. The cycles need one that doesn't. Add it to a per-Kindled row the settle already locks (a `last_seen_at` on `writs` is fine, or a small table of its own: your call). Every holding request refreshes it.
- **Settling,** in the same transaction and locks as §3 of holding.md, for every station with a cycle that isn't faded:
  1. `end = min(now, lastSeen + offlineCapHours, writ.emptyAt)`. No cycles run while the Writ is dark, and none run past the offline cap.
  2. `n = floor((end − cycled_until) / cycleMs)`, never negative.
  3. Credit the n cycles' yield into `basket:<station>` through `inventory.apply`: reason `harvest`, ref `cycle:<kindled>:<station>:<from ms>-<to ms>`, so a stretch pays once. There is no XP.
  4. `cycled_until += n × cycleMs`. If `end < now` (the Writ went dark, or the cap bit), `cycled_until = now`: the lost time and the partial cycle are never paid later, like the active action's lost time.
- **ישן's yield.** Each cycle's bonus in permille is `min(maxPermille, permillePerDay × whole days from virtue_set_at to that cycle's end)`.
  - Credit `floor((carry + quantity × (1000 + bonus)) / 1000)` and keep the remainder in `carry_permille`. All integers; same input, same result.
  - It applies to the basket cycle only, not the active action (GAME DESIGN asked).
  - The count restarts whenever the Virtue is set or changed ("counts its days from the moment it is set").
- **There is no basket capacity:** "192 for a field" is what 48 h of cycle makes under the offline cap, not a stored number (GAME DESIGN).
- **Task 6's seam:** cycle length = `cycleMs` × the station's speed factor, which is 1 today (each piece adds +25% speed in Task 6). Make that one function.
- **`holding:away`** also moves the holding-wide last seen and every `cycled_until` back, so the trickle can be playtested.

### 5. The API

`GET /api/holding` and every holding answer gain:
- `words`: the known word ids;
- `plots`: one entry per layout plot, `{ id, ground, virtue, namedAt, virtueSetAt, kind, level, state }`:
  - `kind` is null while unnamed;
  - `level` is 1 + the pieces standing in the plot, so 1 until Task 6;
  - `state` is `lit` | `dark` | `faded`, from the plot's Writ line;
- per station with a cycle, its `cycle`: `{ cycleMs, cycledUntil, bonusPermille }`, so the client can draw "Next in 12 min" between heartbeats.

`baskets` gains the plots' baskets. The guards refuse unknown fields. The Kindled comes from the session cookie; mutating routes need a JSON body and a same-origin `Origin`. Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` (5).

### 6. Sigils (shared, pure)

`sigilPath(letters, square)` in `packages/shared/src/sigil.ts`, LORE §4.5 "Drawing a sigil":
- each letter reduced to its root 1–9 (the nine chambers: aleph 1 … tet 9, yod 10 → 1 … qof 100 → 1);
- each root found on a 3 × 3 square, in order;
- one line through them, a small circle where the sentence begins and a bar where it ends;
- a repeated letter makes a loop, and two different letters in the same chamber make a short hook.

It returns the cells in order and the marks (start, end, loops, hooks) as data, not pixels. The sentence is one line: the Ground's letters, then the Virtue's.

The square is data in a new `sigil.json`: today Saturn's 3 × 3 (`[[4, 9, 2], [3, 5, 7], [8, 1, 6]]`, Agrippa, LORE's reference), and LORE has been asked to confirm it. Final forms count as their base letter, as the Writ stores them (also asked). Line style by element (LORE): earth heavy and straight, water a long wave, fire jagged, air thin and broken. Draw earth only in M2, and pass the style through so ART can add the others.
